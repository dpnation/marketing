One rubric, every signal

Every signal, from any source, is scored on five dimensions before it can reach the brief. One common rubric is what makes very different signal types — competitor moves, market trends, analyst reports — comparable enough to rank against each other in a single brief. Thresholds are a first draft, expected to be retuned once a few real weeks of output exist.

Strategic Relevance Competitive Impact Customer Relevance Urgency Marketing Actionability

The Weekly Brief

weekly-market-intelligence-brief reads all three signal files every Wednesday — competitive (confirmed and pending review), market, and analyst — scores each entry against the shared rubric above, and writes one artifact: reports/YYYY-MM-DD-market-intelligence.md. First scheduled run: 8/26/2026.

Built to reason across signals, not generate them — keeping it thin stops it from drifting into duplicating what the specialist sensors already do.

What it deliberately doesn't do:

  • Doesn't re-research competitor stories — that's the scan's job
  • Doesn't generate or edit battlecards — recommends a refresh only
  • Doesn't post to Slack or build a dashboard
  • Doesn't auto-promote a discovery candidate into the watchlist
Downstream Actions — Recommended, Not Executed
battlecard-builder

Battlecard Refresh

The brief can recommend a refresh as an action item when a competitive signal is significant enough to warrant one. It never generates, edits, or refreshes a battlecard directly — that stays a firm boundary so battlecard-builder owns its own formatting logic.

Recommendation only

Watchlist Promotion

Where an approved discovery candidate ends up once it's treated as a confirmed competitor to track. Auto-promotion risks polluting the watchlist with noise, so Dana's judgment call is the intended gate, not an afterthought.

Manual — Dana's call
Sales Enablement

Enablement Flag

Signals scored high on "requires Sales action" get called out for Sales Enablement. A recommendation for a human handoff, not an integration — it doesn't post to Slack or push into a sales tool on its own.

Routed, not automated
