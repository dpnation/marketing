Marketing Intelligence Agent — How the Signals Orbit Together

One reasoning layer, three weekly sensors. Competitive, market, and analyst signals feed a shared scoring gate every week; a fourth source (competitor discovery) runs pending review, not confirmed intel. Downstream, the Wednesday brief recommends action — it never executes it. This map shows how the pieces relate, not a workflow to follow step by step.

How to read this

The inner ring is the weekly rhythm, and it's three tasks, not three sources: weekly-competitive-scan (Mon) and weekly-competitor-discovery-scan (Tue) both write to the same file — Competitive analysis/data/latest-signals.json — one confirming known-competitor moves, the other surfacing new candidates at lower confidence. weekly-market-intelligence-scan is the third weekly source, covering industry shifts beyond named competitors. All three run every week without exception.

Discovery output stays lower-confidence, not lower-priority — signals land marked pending_review and aren't treated as confirmed intel until Dana approves a candidate into the watchlist. That approval step is manual on purpose.

analyst_sensor.py is the one source that isn't weekly — it only runs when Dana drops a Gartner or Forrester export in and triggers it. Most weeks it contributes nothing, which is expected, not a gap in coverage.

Every signal, regardless of source, passes through the same scoring gate before it can reach the brief — five 0–5 dimensions summed to 0–25 and mapped to a tier. A signal only earns a place in the brief if it changes positioning, competitive dynamics, customer behavior, or requires action — "no action" is an acceptable answer, and often the correct one.

Three actions sit downstream of the brief, not on it: a battlecard refresh recommendation (built by battlecard-builder, never edited by this agent), a watchlist promotion (manual, Dana's call, never automatic), and a sales enablement flag (routed to Sales Enablement). The brief recommends action — it never executes it. A fourth layer, internal marketing context (V2), is on the roadmap but not yet built.

The Agents, In Detail

What each piece actually does, why it's scoped that way, and — just as important — what it deliberately doesn't do.

The Hub
weekly-marketing-intelligence-brief

Weekly Brief & Scoring

What it is

Reads all three signal sources plus the discovery scan every Wednesday, scores each signal against the shared 5-dimension rubric, and writes one artifact: reports/YYYY-MM-DD-marketing-intelligence.md.

Why it's scoped this way

Built to reason across signals, not generate them — keeping it thin stops it from drifting into duplicating what the specialist sensors already do.

What it doesn't do

Doesn't re-research competitor stories, generate battlecards, post to Slack, build a dashboard, or auto-promote discovery candidates. First run is 8/26 — not yet proven end to end.

Core Weekly Sources
weekly-competitive-scan

Competitive Scan

What it is

Runs Monday ~8:09am against the 10 known competitors and confirms real changes — pricing, positioning, product moves.

Why it's scoped this way

Lives in the sibling Competitive analysis project on purpose — competitor research is a distinct sensor with its own baseline files, not something the brief re-derives.

What it doesn't do

Doesn't score or prioritize what it finds — that judgment call belongs to the brief downstream.

weekly-competitor-discovery-scan

Competitor Discovery

What it is

Runs Tuesday ~8:06am, right alongside the Monday competitive scan — same file, same weekly cadence, but scanning for new or emerging competitors instead of confirming known ones.

Why it's scoped this way

Paired tightly with the competitive scan rather than treated as a separate, lower-priority track — it's weekly and non-negotiable, just lower-confidence by nature. Candidates land marked pending_review: true.

What it doesn't do

Never auto-promotes a candidate into watchlist.json — that step is explicitly manual, Dana's call.

weekly-market-intelligence-scan

Market Intelligence Scan

What it is

A broader industry and category-level scan, Monday ~8:20am, separate from named-competitor tracking.

Why it's scoped this way

Market shifts — regulatory, buyer behavior, category trends — don't map cleanly onto any one competitor profile, so it's a standalone source rather than folded into the competitive scan.

What it doesn't do

Hasn't run yet as of this build — first run is 8/24. Treat early output as unproven until a few cycles land.

Ad Hoc / Event-Driven
analyst_sensor.py

Analyst Intelligence

What it is

The one source that's not on the weekly schedule. Dana drops a Gartner/Forrester export into inputs/analyst/inbox/ and runs the script by hand. Already produced one live signal — Applause confirmed as a Gartner Representative Vendor.

Why it's scoped this way

Analyst report cycles are irregular and human-triggered by nature — forcing it onto the Mon/Tue/Wed cadence would mean scanning for reports that usually aren't there.

What it doesn't do

Doesn't run automatically and contributes nothing most weeks — that's expected behavior, not a coverage gap.

Cross-Cutting
schema/signal-schema.md

Scoring Gate

What it is

The shared rubric — 5 dimensions (strategic relevance, competitive impact, customer relevance, urgency, marketing actionability), 0–5 each, summed to 0–25, mapped to a tier.

Why it's scoped this way

One common rubric is what makes very different signal types — competitor moves, market trends, analyst reports — comparable enough to rank against each other in a single brief.

What it doesn't do

Isn't final — thresholds are a first draft, expected to be retuned once a few real weeks of output exist.

Downstream Actions
battlecard-builder

Battlecard Refresh

What it is

A recommendation the brief can surface when a competitive signal is significant enough to warrant updating a card.

Why it's scoped this way

Keeps a firm boundary between systems — battlecard-builder owns its own formatting logic; the brief shouldn't reimplement it.

What it doesn't do

Never generates, edits, or refreshes a battlecard directly. Recommendation only.

watchlist.json

Watchlist Promotion

What it is

Where an approved discovery candidate ends up once it's treated as a confirmed competitor to track.

Why it's scoped this way

Auto-promotion risks polluting the watchlist with noise — Dana's judgment call is the intended gate, not an afterthought.

What it doesn't do

Nothing here happens automatically. The brief can flag a candidate; it never writes to the file itself.

Sales Enablement

Enablement Flag

What it is

Signals scored high on "requires Sales enablement" get called out for Sales Enablement.

Why it's scoped this way

Sales enablement signals drive what Dana prioritizes next — surfacing them clearly in the brief is the point of scoring for actionability at all.

What it doesn't do

Doesn't post to Slack or push into a sales tool — it's a recommendation for a human handoff, not an integration.
