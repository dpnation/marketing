Quality Assurance vs. Quality Control: QA and QC Explained

It's not enough for companies to simply engage in digital transformation. From payments to insurance documentation to retail shopping, everything is digital today. Thus, businesses must deliver exceptional digital experiences that ensure user retention, customer loyalty and a constant revenue stream — this requires a focus on digital quality.

There are two primary ways that organizations approach digital quality: quality assurance (QA) and quality control (QC). While they have similar intentions, QA and QC take different approaches to ensure customers receive a high-quality product. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between quality assurance and quality control?

Quality assurance (QA) is proactive and process-focused, working throughout the software development life cycle to prevent defects. Quality control (QC) is reactive and product-focused, identifying defects in a completed product, typically during review and testing phases.

Are QA and QC the same thing?

No, though they share the same ultimate goal of a high-quality product. QA establishes policies and procedures across the organization; QC is a subset of QA that tests the finished product against those standards.

Is quality control part of quality assurance?

Yes. Quality control is a subset of quality assurance — QA is the organization-wide framework, and QC is the team-level activity that verifies a specific product meets it.

Does Applause provide digital quality assurance services?

Yes. Applause provides digital quality assurance and QA testing services, helping organizations establish a quality strategy tailored to their specific geographic and market needs.

Maintaining Quality From Start to Finish

While there are elements that divide quality assurance vs. quality control, the two approaches depend on each other to be efficient. Without efficient QA, QC will be inundated with defects, many of which will escape into customers' hands. Without effective QC, the organization can't verify that the latest iteration of a product works as intended and is well-received by customers.

QA and QC help businesses realize a variety of goals that ultimately benefit the business' bottom line. These two digital quality approaches, when done well, help businesses create high-quality products, optimize how teams work, improve customer experiences and loyalty, reduce unnecessary costs and wasted efforts, and iterate faster and more confidently.
