4 Digital Health Trends That Will Define Healthcare in 2026

Healthcare and health tech organizations face increasing pressure to deliver measurable outcomes while providing stronger data protections and a more inclusive patient experience. Patient expectations and demands are also on the rise for their digital experiences. They want accuracy, ease of use, personalization and trust. Regulators are paying close attention to ensure that health technology is secure and reliable across every device and demographic.

Here are four trends that will shape digital health this year, and how real-world testing — validating products and workflows with real users and real devices before they reach patients — can help teams prepare for each one.

The healthcare and health tech organizations who succeed in their digital initiatives will be the ones who move beyond lab testing to capture and implement key performance insights.

4. Accessibility testing becomes a core requirement

(Renamed from "Accessibility becomes a core requirement." Population stats and virtual-care adoption figures carry over unchanged.)

High-quality products must account for potential accessibility and usability challenges, lest they risk leaving entire populations without access to tools that are essential for health management. Designing products solely for able-bodied users — whether intentional or not — is not only discriminatory, but decreases reach and patient satisfaction as well.

True accessibility requires health tech teams to run usability testing with real users with disabilities. Can a blind user navigate a telehealth appointment with a screen reader? Can a person with mobility challenges successfully refill a prescription through an app? Do digital health tools work effectively with assistive devices? Empathy studies with real people with disabilities help identify usability barriers that cannot be found with artificial testing. When you can test real products with real users, it helps ensure usability for the broadest patient population possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should health tech teams evaluate when testing condition-specific wearables?

Whether patients can set up and use the device correctly without clinical supervision, whether it performs accurately during exercise and across different body types and skin tones, and whether it keeps working through real-world conditions like battery drain, connectivity interruptions and software updates — not just in a lab setting with a small group of volunteers.

How does real-world testing reveal accessibility issues that lab testing misses?

By testing with real users who have auditory, cognitive, motor or visual impairments — such as whether a blind user can navigate a telehealth appointment with a screen reader, or a person with mobility challenges can refill a prescription through an app. Empathy studies with real people with disabilities help identify usability barriers that artificial testing can't find.

What is real-world testing, and how does it differ from lab testing for health tech products?

Real-world testing validates products and workflows with real users and real devices, under the actual conditions patients use them in, rather than a closed lab environment with a small set of volunteers. Applause helps support healthcare innovators by enabling real-world testing at scale, sourcing targeted users who represent an organization's actual patient base.

Why does regulatory scrutiny make real-world testing important for health data privacy?

Lab environments often fail to capture how users actually interact with products handling sensitive health data — patients may not update device software regularly or may have compromised encryption key management. Real-world testing with real users helps reveal these gaps and make security features more usable and transparent, supporting compliance.
